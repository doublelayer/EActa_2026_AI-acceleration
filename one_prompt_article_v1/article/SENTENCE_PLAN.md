# Article-specific paragraph and sentence functions

These are functional slots filled from the prompt's facts and evidence. They prescribe the logic, not exact article sentences. Combine or divide slots for natural prose while preserving their order and purpose. Use the same central terms and authorial voice throughout. The article contains connected paragraphs rather than visible lists.

## Abstract: one paragraph, about eight sentences, maximum 250 words

1. Establish accelerating catalyst discovery with artificial intelligence, including scientific communication alongside catalyst screening.
2. Introduce our real ORR case study on molecular DASCs as the setting for the demonstration.
3. Connect the DFT investigation of 88 catalysts to ridge-model predictions for 1,260 additional structures; distinguish calculated evidence from predictions.
4. Give one concise supported family-level result, using Fe and S-containing structures, to establish scientific substance.
5. State that we guided an LLM to prepare the complete article from verified scientific inputs and explicit writing guidance.
6. Explain that repeated scientific and editorial review developed the guidance; keep development effort visible without procedural inventory.
7. Identify the complete article itself as the manuscript-generation demonstration under our guidance.
8. Resolve the opening through ML screening and scientific writing, with the consequence that researchers can concentrate on scientific questions and methods.

Avoid computational parameter lists, individual catalyst rankings, descriptor counts, data-point bookkeeping and new time-saving estimates. The contribution hierarchy is more important than a fixed sentence count.

## Introduction: seven connected paragraphs

### Paragraph 1: the acceleration question

1. Echo the title and connect production of scientific results with their communication.
2. Explain how DFT calculations and ML predictions contribute to candidate selection.
3. Explain why writing is a further stage before results become available for examination and reuse.
4. Support the months-long publication context briefly with the Björk–Solomon and Ding–Du evidence; distinguish publication intervals from unmeasured writing time in the evidence map.

### Paragraph 2: thesis and editorial context

1. Attribute to the selected editorials the importance of a coherent theme, selected figures and clear interpretation.
2. State our agreement that readers need the evidence and its meaning explained.
3. Question how much publication depends on storytelling skill when scientific value rests on methods and data.
4. State the data-first thesis directly with our agency.
5. Connect the thesis to guided LLM manuscript preparation as the case-study test.

### Paragraph 3: scientific writing is teachable

1. Establish that scientific-writing conventions are taught to early-stage researchers, citing the supplied writing article/course references.
2. Explain how we applied article, paragraph and evidence conventions to the LLM.
3. Explain how these instructions supported writing while we developed and checked the scientific argument.

### Paragraph 4: reusable data

1. Connect delegation of writing to scientific data that can be examined and reused.
2. Explain that other researchers can combine datasets and ask additional questions, with the FAIR citation.
3. Contrast the structures and values preserved in a database with an article's selected analysis.
4. State the consequence for independent scientific questions only if the previous sentences have not already made it explicit.

### Paragraph 5: guided LLM precedent

1. Connect LLM scientific text to the evidence it must communicate.
2. Use the vocabulary study and narrative caution only to motivate faithful communication; avoid unsupported fraud statistics.
3. Introduce Ifargan's data-to-paper precedent and its traceable outputs.
4. Identify our contribution as a complete electrochemistry article from verified DFT, ML, figures and supplied knowledge.

### Paragraph 6: writing process and Figure 1

1. Introduce the seven-stage writing figure.
2. Explain our use of the LLM throughout manuscript preparation and authorized code assistance.
3. Show our guidance and scientific checking as concrete actions rather than another responsibility list.

### Paragraph 7: the completed study

1. Introduce the three connected stages as one research process.
2. State what the DFT stage establishes and how ML extends screening, using the main cohort counts only if needed.
3. State the complete LLM article-generation achievement.
4. Connect both acceleration modes to the opening question. Omit an inventory of what each later figure shows.

## Conclusions: three paragraphs

### Paragraph 1: principal achievement

1. State our use of a guided LLM to prepare the complete scientific article from verified research inputs.
2. Echo accelerating catalyst discovery with artificial intelligence and identify scientific writing alongside catalyst screening as the demonstrated routes.

### Paragraph 2: genuine scientific basis

1. State what the DFT investigation of 88 molecular catalysts established.
2. Connect the 1,260-structure ML extension to supported Fe and S-containing catalyst families for further investigation.

### Paragraph 3: synthesis

1. Explain how scientific-writing conventions guided manuscript preparation by the LLM.
2. Return to the Introduction's thesis: scientific methods and data establish the contribution, and guided AI can undertake its communication under scientific oversight.
3. End with the concrete opportunity for researchers to concentrate on scientific questions and accelerate catalyst discovery with AI assistance in screening and writing.

Use the same terms as the title and Introduction. Add no new scientific result, citation, quantitative productivity claim or administrative checklist. Use full terms where the selected conclusion style calls for them; do not mechanically duplicate the Abstract.
