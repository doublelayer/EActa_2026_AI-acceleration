P00006  Abstract
P00007  Accelerating catalyst discovery with artificial intelligence (AI) requires accurate density functional theory (DFT) calculations, useful machine learning (ML) predictions, and clear scientific writing. We demonstrate this with the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) on molecular dual-atom site catalysts (DASCs). DFT calculations for 88 molecular catalysts identified three structures with the lowest calculated ORR overpotentials of [[INSERT id=0; author=OpenAI Codex; date=2026-10-13T00:00:00Z: 0.29, ]]0.34, [[DELETE id=1; author=OpenAI Codex; date=2026-10-13T00:00:00Z: 0.39, ]]and [[DELETE id=2; author=OpenAI Codex; date=2026-10-13T00:00:00Z: 0.41 ]][[INSERT id=2; author=OpenAI Codex; date=2026-10-13T00:00:00Z: 0.36 ]]V. P00012  1. Introduction
P00013  Independent analyses place chemistry publication timelines at several months after submission. One journal sample reported an average interval of approximately nine months, while a study of chemistry articles published by the Public Library of Science reported a median submission to publication interval of 154 days [10.1016/j.joi.2013.09.001; 10.1007/s11192-023-04640-6]. These measurements begin at submission and exclude preparation of the first manuscript and revisions after rejection. Manuscript preparation therefore adds time before the publication interval even begins.
P00014  Editors have argued that a coherent scientific story and carefully selected figures help readers understand a paper [10.1038/nphys724; 10.1021/acsenergylett.8b02322; 10.1021/acsenergylett.1c02401; 10.1038/s41551-018-0202-5]. Clear writing supports research, but writing skill alone does not establish scientific value. Our thesis is direct: researchers can delegate article writing to a guided LLM when DFT calculations, ML predictions, and databases constrain every claim. The story must follow the data.
P00015  Catalyst databases can support analyses that their creators did not anticipate. Researchers can combine datasets, re-evaluate data points, and test new interpretations [10.1038/sdata.2016.18]. A database preserves molecular structures and calculated values; each article presents specific analyses of those data. Reusable databases let researchers test further questions against the same scientific results.
P00016  Vocabulary analyses of published abstracts have documented changes associated with LLM assisted writing [10.1126/sciadv.adt3813]. Editorials also warn that story driven selection can hide conflicting observations [10.1038/nmeth.2726]. Ifargan and colleagues showed that interacting LLM agents can prepare human verifiable papers from annotated data [10.1056/aioa2400555]. We build on this work by supplying the LLM with verified DFT calculations, ML predictions, three databases, literature, figures, and project instructions.

P00020  Our study links three stages. DFT calculations establish adsorption energetics for 88 molecular catalysts. ML predictions extend the analysis to 1176 optimized molecular structures and identify catalyst features associated with lower predicted overpotentials. A guided LLM then prepares the complete article from the verified results. Figure 2 shows the molecular variables, Figure 3 presents DFT scaling relations and ORR thermodynamics, and Figure 4 presents the corresponding ML predictions. Together, these results show how artificial intelligence can accelerate catalyst discovery through DFT calculations, ML predictions, and scientific writing.

P00022  2.1. Catalyst structures
P00023  The molecular catalysts were assembled from the components shown in Figure 2. Our previous data article defined B1 as the 1,2-phenylenediamine fragment and B2 as a 1,8-naphthalenediamine fragment [10.1016/j.dib.2026.112806]. Each molecule contains one dual-atom site with two metal-atom adsorption centers. The two metal positions were occupied by Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, or Cu, and each of the two bridge positions contained N, O, or S. Every clean and adsorbate structure in the DFT analysis was fully optimized. Three linked molecular catalyst databases were used: the published structure database, the fixed DFT dataset of 88 catalysts, and the larger dataset of 1176 optimized catalysts used for ML predictions. Each optimized structure and its energy were stored in an Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE) database with the catalyst name, metal identities, ligand components, and charge metadata. The ads_site field identifies the metal-atom adsorption center occupied by each intermediate.
P00024  2.2. DFT calculations and adsorption structures
P00025  Spin polarized DFT calculations were performed with GPAW 25.7 and ASE 3.28.0 software [10.1063/5.0182685; 10.1088/1361-648X/aa680e]. The projector augmented wave method, revised Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof functional, and D4 dispersion correction were used [10.1063/1.5090222]. A plane wave cutoff of 550 eV, real space grid spacing of 0.16 Å, and 6.00 Å of vacuum were applied. Each atomic structure was optimized until the maximum force fell below 0.05 eV Å⁻¹, and only calculations that converged with these parameters entered the fixed DFT set. The setup follows earlier work on related molecular catalysts [10.26434/chemrxiv.15001712/v1]. The total charge was set to +2 for every modeled catalyst, including N bridged structures. This consistent cohort represents one charge assignment and the spin states reached by the spin polarized optimizations. A broader electronic structure survey should compare alternative charges, mixed oxidation states, and spin states.

P00052  2.6. LLM manuscript generation

P00079  3.5. Alternative activity descriptors and reusable DFT data
P00080  The same DFT calculations, matched by catalyst and adsorption center, support several thermodynamic analyses of ORR. The conventional CHE overpotential is set by the least favorable individual reduction step. Calle-Vallejo's electrochemical step symmetry index evaluates how evenly the four step potentials divide the 1.23 V reaction free energy, while Exner's free energy span identifies the largest cumulative uphill change across consecutive reaction states at a selected potential [10.1016/j.coelec.2018.03.025; 10.1021/acscatal.2c03997]. Each descriptor asks a different question of the same adsorption free energies. Because catalyst and adsorption center identities remain attached to every value, other researchers can apply these analyses directly to the same dataset.
P00081  The DFT dataset preserves calculated adsorption energies with the matching catalyst and adsorption center identities. The database structure allows researchers to apply other ORR descriptors and compare their results with the analyses reported here.

P00086  4. Conclusions
P00087  We demonstrate accelerating catalyst discovery with artificial intelligence by using a guided LLM to generate the article from verified DFT calculations, ML predictions, databases, literature, and figures. We directed the investigation and checked the article against its scientific inputs. P00088  DFT calculations for 88 molecular catalysts identified three S bridged structures with low calculated ORR overpotentials. Together, the DFT calculations, ML predictions, and LLM assisted scientific writing demonstrate how artificial intelligence can accelerate catalyst discovery.
