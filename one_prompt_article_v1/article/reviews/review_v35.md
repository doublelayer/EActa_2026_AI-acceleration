P00009  1. Introduction
P00010  Accelerating catalyst discovery and design with AI should make the research itself faster and more reliable. Yet scientific publishing often treats storytelling as the decisive intellectual labor. [[INSERT id=14; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:11Z: Editors from ]]Nature Physics call[[DELETE id=15; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:18Z: s]] the story the principle that should organize an entire paper[[INSERT id=16; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:24Z: ; ]][[INSERT id=16; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:24Z: Editors form]][[DELETE id=16; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:24Z: , ]]ACS Energy Letters ask[[DELETE id=17; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:34Z: s]] authors to build discussion around a coherent story[[INSERT id=18; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:43Z: ;]][[DELETE id=18; author=Vladislav Ivanistsev; date=2026-10-11T19:10:43Z: , and]] other editorials describe figures as the backbone or even the most persuasive part of an article [10.1038/nphys724; 10.1021/acsenergylett.8b02322; 10.1038/s44160-022-00067-8; 10.1038/s41567-021-01332-x]. This is a powerful thesis, and it is the thesis we challenge. P00011  Our counterclaim is direct: researchers need not spend months personally composing that story. When the data are structured, the methods are explicit, and every claim can be traced to evidence, we can outsource much of the analysis and narrative drafting to a guided LLM. The LLM can organize the argument and select a path through the results; we remain responsible for asking the question, producing the data, choosing the analysis, and deciding what the evidence means. That change moves human attention from polishing the story to strengthening the research that makes the story possible.
P00012  The antithesis is already visible. The same fluency that makes an LLM useful can produce an authoritative story from weak evidence, conceal an inconvenient result, or decorate an unsupported claim with a plausible figure. Nature Methods warned that narrative can distort science even before generative AI made polished prose inexpensive [10.1038/nmeth.2726]. We confront that danger by making the data, calculation rules, code, and source links inspectable. In this article, the story must answer to the record.
P00013  We test this argument through the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR). Its four electron pathway depends on the binding of O, OH, and OOH, so correlated adsorption free energies constrain thermodynamic activity [10.1021/jp047349j]. Molecular dual-atom site catalysts give us a defined set in which metal identity, bridge, diamine, and adsorption site vary, see Figure 1. Related molecular catalysts show how binding geometry and the second metal can change oxygen intermediate energetics [10.1021/jacs.4c12705; 10.26434/chemrxiv.15001712/v1]. We follow these changes through the DFT data in Figure 2 and ask what ML retains in Figure 3. The calculations provide the antithesis to an article driven by narrative alone: the LLM can write the complete paper, but the data determine what it is allowed to say.
P00014  2. Computational methods
P00015  2.1. Catalyst structures
P00016  We assembled the molecular catalysts from the components shown in Figure 1. Following the notation introduced in our data article [10.1016/j.dib.2026.112806], A2 denotes the fixed dialdehyde and B1 and B2 denote the two diamines. Each molecule contains two metal centers chosen from Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu and bridge atoms selected from N, O, or S. The same article describes the molecular assembly and initial structure preparation. We fully optimized all structures used in the DFT analysis, including the clean catalysts and structures containing adsorbed intermediates. The clean database used here contains 88 molecular catalysts.
P00017  We retained the catalyst identity, metal positions, ligand components, and charge metadata in the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE) database.
P00018  2.2. DFT calculations
P00019  We performed spin polarized DFT calculations using GPAW 26.7 and ASE 3.29.0 software [10.1063/5.0182685; 10.1088/1361-648X/aa680e]. We used the projector augmented wave method, the revised Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof functional, and the D4 dispersion correction [10.1063/1.5090222]. The calculations used a plane wave cutoff of 550 eV, a real space grid spacing of 0.16 Å, 6.00 Å vacuum, and Γ point sampling for isolated molecules. We fully relaxed the atomic positions and converged the electronic density. We calculated gas phase H₂ and H₂O with the same settings. This methodology follows our work on related molecular catalysts [10.26434/chemrxiv.15001712/v1].
P00020  2.3. Adsorption structures and database matching
P00021  We placed O, OH, and OOH at the metal adsorption sites identified in Figure 1 and fully optimized each structure. For OOH, we retained the lower energy of the two tested initial orientations at each site. We joined all intermediate energies by catalyst name and adsorption site label. Thus, each reaction pathway refers to one specific metal atom in one molecular catalyst.
P00022  We defined adsorption energies from the total energies of the clean catalyst, adsorbed structures, and gas phase references using Eqs. (1)–(3). An asterisk denotes the clean adsorption site or a bound intermediate, as appropriate. The analysis used the supplied corrected adsorption free energies directly for scaling, filtering, and thermodynamic activity estimates.

P00047  2.6. ML regression analysis
P00048  For the ML analysis, we used fitted ridge regression models for OH, O, and OOH adsorption free energies. Their clean catalyst descriptors represent Bader charge and electron population, local spin, and ligand charge distribution. The supplied models contain eight predictors for OH and O and six for OOH. We applied the saved training medians, means, standard deviations, and coefficients to all available catalyst and adsorption site records. We then joined the three predictions at each site, applied the energy filters, and evaluated the overpotential with Eqs. (5)–(7).
P00049  Figure 3 includes every available site that passes these filters, including sites used for model fitting. It describes predicted trends across the library. We used the supplied validation predictions to check reproduction of the saved models; the all-site figure itself does not measure independent predictive accuracy.
P00050  2.7. AI assisted manuscript generation
P00051  We used an LLM accessed through OpenAI Codex to generate the complete manuscript and develop analysis and figure code under our direction. Our motivation was to examine a practical response to the growing use of AI in scientific writing. Analysis of biomedical abstracts documents substantial uptake of LLM assisted writing [10.1126/sciadv.adt3813]. That observation establishes the scale of adoption; it does not establish the reliability of any individual article. We address the risk that rapid generation can also multiply unsupported claims and invented figures by grounding this article in supplied calculations, explicit analysis choices, and identifiable sources.
P00052  We supplied the research question, databases, regression coefficients, literature, and writing instructions, then refined the analysis and text through successive corrections. We required figures to be generated by executable code from the molecular structures and numerical records. We used the LLM to organize the evidence, examine alternative analyses, and compose the narrative; we decided which questions to pursue and checked the resulting claims. The manuscript itself is part of this demonstration. Its coherence is useful only insofar as a reader can follow the account back to the calculations and assess our interpretation.
P00053  3. Results and discussion
P00054  3.1. Molecular design space and site identity
P00055  Figure 1 gives us a common molecular structure in which to compare the effects of changing individual components. Both metal positions draw from the same six elements, while the bridge and diamine provide further variation and the dialdehyde remains fixed. We define an adsorption site as the metal atom within the organic ligands to which an ORR intermediate binds. Keeping that atom identifiable lets us distinguish the two possible pathways in the same molecule.

P00059  3.2. Adsorption scaling and calculated ORR thermodynamics
P00060  Figure 2a compares O and OOH adsorption free energies with OH adsorption free energy. The OOH–OH relation has a slope of 0.69 and an intercept of 2.98 eV; the O–OH relation has a slope of 1.66 and an intercept of 1.20 eV. The adsorption site families occupy different parts of these trends, with scatter around the global relations. Related molecular catalysts can alter OOH binding geometry and adsorption scaling through their paired metal centers [10.1021/jacs.4c12705; 10.1002/adma.202403549]. The present trends provide a basis for examining such structural effects within a common molecular structure.

P00064  3.3. Scaling and activity from regression predictions
P00065  We next ask how much of this pattern the regression recovers. Figure 3 applies the same analysis to predicted adsorption free energies. Complete predictions were available for 148 sites across 88 catalysts, and the filters retained 144 sites across 87 catalysts. The predicted OOH–OH slope and intercept are 0.67 and 2.98 eV, while those for O–OH are 1.73 and 1.14 eV. Their proximity to the DFT relations in Figure 2 shows that the descriptors retain the broad variation across the molecular library. The narrower spread of the predictions also prompts a closer look at the individual rankings.
P00066  Near the volcano maximum, the distinction becomes consequential. The lowest predicted overpotential is 0.30 V at the Fe site of an Fe–Mn catalyst with S bridge atoms and B1 diamine. The DFT analysis in Figure 2 instead prioritizes a Mn site. We can use this disagreement to select a useful next calculation: inspect the binding structures and descriptors of these candidates, then test whether the predicted order survives. Figure 3 therefore helps us direct further research while displaying the broad trends that the regression has learned. Its all-site predictions include fitted records, so the figure describes the represented library rather than establishing transfer to new chemical families.

P00069  3.4. From reusable data to the next research question
P00070  The comparison between Figures 2 and 3 raises a further question: how much does candidate selection depend on the activity measure we choose? We can examine it with the same site matched records. The Calle-Vallejo electrochemical step symmetry index and the Exner free energy span emphasize different features of the reaction energetics [10.1016/j.coelec.2018.03.025; 10.1021/acscatal.2c03997]. Once the energies are stored with their molecular and site identities, we can ask an LLM to implement either analysis, inspect the code, and compare the resulting rankings. We reserve new DFT calculations for questions that change the geometry, charge state, or adsorption structure.
P00071  We will release the structures and results as an ASE database and machine readable JSON, together with code that reconstructs the analysis. These formats support findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable data practices [10.1038/sdata.2016.18]. They also make our interpretation open to revision: another researcher can choose a different descriptor, regenerate the figures, and identify where the conclusions change. The value of the record grows when it can answer a question that we did not originally ask.
P00072  We developed the instructions over about two weeks and estimate that executing the resulting instructions to generate an integrated draft takes less than an hour. In our experience, composing comparable articles manually has taken months. This account describes our project rather than a controlled timing study. The concrete result is that we can revise an analysis and its written explanation together, while directing our attention to the assumptions and decisions that determine what the result means.
P00073  4. Conclusions
P00074  Accelerating catalyst discovery and design with AI includes accelerating how we analyze and report the research. Molecular dual-atom site catalysts provide the case study: the same structured records support adsorption scaling, thermodynamic rankings, ML predictions, and alternative activity descriptors. A guided LLM can execute these analyses, expose disagreements among their rankings, and explain why those disagreements matter. Each disagreement becomes a question for further research rather than a reason to force the results into one story.
P00075  We began with the claim that a convincing story is central to a scientific paper, and we deliberately outsourced the composition of that story to an LLM. The LLM generated the complete article, connected the figures, and revised the argument under our direction. It could do so because the calculations, molecular identities, analysis rules, and sources constrained the prose. This is how AI can accelerate catalyst discovery and design: researchers spend less time manufacturing narrative continuity and more time planning the research, producing reliable data, testing alternative interpretations, and verifying the final claims. The synthesis is neither story without science nor data without explanation. It is researcher directed science in which an LLM carries much of the analytical and writing load while the researchers remain responsible for what the work establishes.

We began with the claim that a convincing story is central to a scientific paper, and we deliberately outsourced the composition of that story to an LLM. The LLM generated the complete article, connected the figures, and revised the argument under our direction. It could do so because the calculations, molecular identities, analysis rules, and sources constrained the prose. This is how AI can accelerate catalyst discovery and design: researchers spend less time manufacturing narrative continuity and more time planning the research, producing reliable data, testing alternative interpretations, and verifying the final claims.
