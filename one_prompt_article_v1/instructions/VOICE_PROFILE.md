# Chandler–Feynman voice profile

## Scope

Use American English and the qualitative balance of 70% Chandler precision and 30% Feynman clarity and engagement. These percentages are not quotas. This selectable profile determines voice, not scientific facts or the article's thesis. Obtain those from the article packet. Use SECTION_FUNCTIONS.md for rhetorical structure and the selected terminology file for exact terms.

## Voice and agency

Write as researchers who understand the evidence and guide a colleague through its meaning. Show agency through a scientific choice, evidence, and consequence. Use we and our naturally outside Methods; use passive voice consistently in Methods when the article contract requires it. Do not refer to the present authors in the third person.

Resolve the actor sentence by sentence. Use we and our for the present authors' work. Use researchers or scientists for general statements about the scientific community. A transition from a general practice to the present study also requires a clear change of subject. Preserve this distinction during every editing pass.

Chandler influence means direct language, precise observation, confident reasoning, and a consistent voice. Feynman influence means concrete explanation and attention to physical meaning. Neither requires literary imitation, invented emotion, rhetorical questions, conversational catchphrases, jokes, or declarations of surprise.

## Paragraphs and sentences

Give each paragraph one purpose and make each sentence develop it. Vary sentence length according to reasoning; impose no quotas or mandatory long sentences. Keep subordinate clauses only when they express a dependency. Do not disguise task lists as semicolon-separated prose. End a paragraph when its argument is complete.

Repeat required terms instead of substituting synonyms. Echo the selected title's terminology where the article contract requires it. Prefer concrete subjects and direct verbs. Reserve calculate for calculations; use evaluate or estimate for the corresponding analytical operations. Preserve a physical quantity as subject when scientifically necessary, but do not attribute agency to abstract quantities.

Stop a paragraph when its scientific purpose is complete. Add a closing consequence only when it advances the reasoning or prepares the next paragraph. Keep necessary contrasts, including rather, when they express a real distinction. Determine typography by meaning: an element symbol and a physical-quantity symbol can use the same letter with different formatting.

## Restraint

State positively what the evidence establishes. Preserve limitations that change scientific interpretation; remove defensive disclaimers and empty contrast formulas. Use however for a genuine gap and yet for a qualification when needed, without mechanically inserting either. Avoid nontechnical gerund constructions, unsupported comparisons, metaphors, needless hyphens, and em dashes. Preserve technical names and literal source titles.

## Generation and revision

Keep voice consistent while changing the scientific job of each section. The Introduction explains why the study was undertaken; Methods describe its reproducible procedures; Results explain what the evidence shows; Discussion develops its meaning; Conclusions answer the opening question. Engagement comes from that connected explanation. Keep exact scientific terms stable across all five jobs.

In GENERATE mode, compose original prose from facts and sentence functions. In EDIT mode, preserve sound author wording and change only what the request or a documented defect requires. The latest author instruction controls within scientific evidence. Never use a sample paragraph or earlier manuscript as a prose template in the one-prompt test.
