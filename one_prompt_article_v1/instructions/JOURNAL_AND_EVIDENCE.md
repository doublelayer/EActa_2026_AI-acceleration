## Evidence policy

Ground every literature-derived scientific claim in a DOI-linked knowledge base or an author-supplied source. Keep the source association attached while extracting, paraphrasing, combining, and moving claims.

Distinguish:

- what one source directly reports;
- a synthesis supported by several sources;
- an inference proposed for the present project;
- a result produced by the present databases.

Do not use a preprint as peer-reviewed evidence. Label publication status accurately.

## Source routing

Use the input manifest and source index to locate the literature selected for the article. Topic-specific summaries supply source-linked context; the article evidence map identifies the claims actually needed. For original wording, exact methods, or numerical results, inspect the supplied original source. During isolated generation, the declared allowlist defines the accessible literature.

## Reliable extraction sequence

1. Identify the exact source and DOI.
2. Record the passage location or figure/table context.
3. Extract the scientific claim without removing limitations or conditions.
4. Classify the claim as source result, synthesis, or project inference.
5. Paraphrase independently.
6. Verify the paraphrase against the source and retained qualifiers.
7. Attach the DOI before moving the sentence into manuscript prose.

This sequence provides the traceability of a Zotero highlight workflow without requiring Zotero.

## Citation form by manuscript state

- Preserve the DOI as the source key while researching, drafting, and auditing claims.
- Obtain citation presentation and any drafting exceptions from the article packet. Preserve the selected convention through editing and document construction; convert or reformat references only when the task authorizes it.
- Retain complete bibliographic metadata and DOI links in the reference list.
- Attach each citation to the specific claim it supports and follow the selected punctuation convention. Keep multiple sources grouped only when each supports the same attached claim; give different claims their own source associations.

## Prohibited evidence transfer

- Do not cite a literature paper for the present database construction, GPAW settings, matching rules, filters, regression choices, or numerical results unless it is truly the source of that choice.
- Do not copy source wording, numerical values, computational settings, or conclusions merely because the present article follows a similar structure.
- Do not smooth over conflicting evidence. State the disagreement or narrow the claim.
- Do not infer experimental validation from density functional theory agreement or regression performance.
