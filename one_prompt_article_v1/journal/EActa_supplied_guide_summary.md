## Journal fit and article type

Electrochimica Acta publishes original electrochemical research, including computational, theoretical, artificial intelligence, and machine learning studies when the primary contribution advances electrochemistry. Electrocatalysis and molecular electrochemistry are within scope. A report of calculations, materials, or performance alone is insufficient without new insight into electrochemical processes or phenomena. A Research Paper must be a complete, authoritative, clearly presented account with special significance and general interest. The journal gives 8–12 printed pages and 5–10 figures as typical ranges, not mandatory limits. Combine related results where this improves concise presentation; supplementary material can carry extensive datasets and additional analyses.

## Article structure and presentation

Use clearly defined, numbered sections and subsections. A conventional Research Paper sequence is Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, Experimental or Computational Methods, Results and Discussion, Conclusions, declarations, and References. Keep the abstract unnumbered. Place computational procedures and equations needed for reproducibility in the main Methods section. Results and Discussion should interpret results critically and in the order figures appear. Conclusions should summarize findings and their significance, then state relevant remaining challenges.

The Introduction must state the purpose and explain how the work advances beyond the state of the art. Avoid generic introductions and literature lists that do not establish a specific research gap. Keep claims factual and supported. Avoid subjective or exaggerated phrases such as “superior performance” and “first ever.”

## Title page, abstract, and submission-only components

- Title page: concise title; accurate given and family names; full institutional names and postal addresses including country; affiliation markers as lower-case superscript letters; one corresponding author with current email; present address as a numbered footnote when applicable. Obtain any author-selected drafting exceptions from the article packet and record them in the requirement map.
- Abstract: factual and self-contained, no more than 250 words; state purpose, basic methods, principal findings and conclusions, and what is new. Avoid references and uncommon abbreviations.
- Keywords: 1–7 English terms; avoid multiword phrases and abbreviations where possible.
- Highlights: required at submission as a separate editable file; 3–5 bullets, each no more than 85 characters including spaces. Emphasize electrochemical insight, not merely tasks or performance.
- Graphical abstract: required as a separate file; target 531 × 1328 pixels (height × width) or proportional, readable at 5 × 13 cm; preferred TIFF, EPS, PDF, or MS Office. Disclose generative AI use in its caption if AI generated artwork is used. Do not invent an image from primary research data.
- Editable manuscript: DOCX or TeX source; Word must be single column. PDF alone is not an acceptable source file. Embed figures at relevant positions, and also supply each figure separately. Supply figure captions in the manuscript and in a separate captions file.

## Methods, equations, tables, and figures

Methods must provide sufficient detail to reproduce the calculations and analysis. Cite established procedures and explain substantial changes. Equations must be editable text, variables italicized, simple formulae inline where practical, small fractions written with a solidus, and displayed equations numbered consecutively when referenced. Tables must be editable, cited in text, numbered in order, captioned, and free of vertical rules or shading; avoid duplicating prose.

Cite each figure in order. Captions need a brief title and concise panel description and must define symbols or abbreviations. Use separate logical figure files; recommended vector types are EPS or PDF, with fonts embedded or text converted to paths. Raster minimums include 300 dpi for photos and 1000 dpi for bitmapped line drawings. Combined panels are allowed when comparison is intended. Keep image adjustments truthful; do not introduce, remove, move, or obscure data features. AI may generate explanatory diagrams and reproducible, data-derived visualizations, but not substitute or manipulate primary observed data.

## Data and research outputs

The Guide applies Option C of the journal research data policy: deposit research data in a relevant repository, cite and link the dataset in the article, or explain why it cannot be shared. Include a Data Availability statement. Dataset references should identify creators, title, repository, version, year, and persistent identifier; add `[dataset]` before the reference-list entry. Cite software as software when relevant. Do not describe planned files as already deposited.

## Declarations and authorship

- Name one corresponding author.
- Include a competing-interest declaration; authors with none may state that they have nothing to declare.
- Disclose funding and the role of sponsors. Use a separate Funding statement when applicable. Put acknowledgments in a separate section directly before References.
- Include CRediT roles for applicable authors.
- Include a generative AI and AI assisted technologies declaration before References when such tools were used to prepare the manuscript. Identify the tool and purpose; the human authors must verify, edit, and take responsibility for the content. AI systems must never be listed as authors or coauthors.
- Authorship changes after submission require editor approval and written agreement from all authors.

## Citation style

In text, use sequential Arabic reference numbers in square brackets, assigned in order of first appearance. Place the citation before the sentence’s comma or period, for example, `as demonstrated [3,6].` Number the reference list in the same order. Use consistent, complete bibliographic information: authors, article title, journal, year, volume, pages or article number, and DOI where available. Abbreviate journal names using LTWA. Every in-text citation must appear in References and vice versa. Cite real, verified sources only; clearly identify preprints. Web references require the full URL and access date.

## Required pre-submission materials and checks

Prepare highlights, graphical abstract, separate figure files, a separate captions file, repository deposit and data citation, cover letter, competing-interest document, and any other portal forms. The cover letter should be concise and explain fit, aim, main electrochemical insight, and novelty; omit funding, declarations, and reviewer suggestions. Verify that all references match, permissions are secured, and spelling is checked. Confirm ethics statements only if relevant to the research.

Before submission, audit scientific values, exact catalyst–site matching, OOH orientation choice, figures, equations, author details, corresponding-author requirements and drafting exceptions, declarations, reference order, dataset availability, and all required upload components. Human authors verify the scientific content, approve the submitted text, and remain accountable for it. Record actual review and revision activity from evidence.
